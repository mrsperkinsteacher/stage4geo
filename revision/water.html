Real-world case study to revise with

The Murray-Darling Basin

Australia's own case study for the value of water (Ch.15) and water scarcity and management (Ch.14) - one river system, several very different values placed on the same water:

Economic value - the Basin produces around a third of Australia's food supply, with agriculture (especially irrigation) the biggest single user of its water.
Cultural/spiritual value - Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples' connection to the Basin's rivers and wetlands goes back tens of thousands of years, and water management today increasingly includes Aboriginal water rights and cultural flows (Ch.15.3).
Scarcity pressures - over-allocation between states, drought and climate change have all strained the system, making it a real example of competing water users having to be managed together (Ch.14.1-14.2).

Good to contrast against the textbook's other two water case studies: Mawsynram, India (the world's wettest place - too much water) and the Gordon Dam, Tasmania (water stored and managed for hydropower).
