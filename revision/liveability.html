Place and Liveability

What makes a place liveable, how perceptions of liveability differ, and what can be done to enhance it.

Key vocabulary

LiveabilityA measure of what a place is like to live in, based on a range of factors.
Factors affecting liveabilityThe reasons that make a place more or less enjoyable to live in - environmental, social, economic and political.
PerceptionA person's own view of how liveable a place is, shaped by their experiences, culture and what they value.
Access to servicesHow easily people can reach things they need, like healthcare, education and transport.
Environmental qualityHow clean, healthy and hazard-free an environment is - affected by pollution, land degradation and natural hazards.
Social connectednessThe relationships people have with others, and how strongly they feel part of a community.
InfrastructureThe facilities and services a community needs to function, e.g. roads, education and healthcare.
Enhancing liveabilityActions taken by individuals, community groups, councils or governments to make a place more liveable.
Urban sprawlThe spread of a city outward into new low-density suburbs, often far from jobs and services.
Natural surveillanceBeing able to see public spaces from homes, shops, streets and parks - a design feature that improves perceived safety.
Real-world case study to revise with

Gosford vs the Global Liveability Index

The same comparison used in class: our own local area against the Economist Intelligence Unit's Global Liveability Index, which scores 173 cities worldwide on stability, healthcare, culture and environment, education, and infrastructure (Ch.6).

  1. The 2025 top 5 - Copenhagen (1st), Vienna and Zurich (tied 2nd), Melbourne (4th), Geneva (5th). Two more Australian cities also made the top 10 - Sydney (6th) and Adelaide (9th).
  2. Near the bottom - Damascus was ranked least liveable in 2025, with Tripoli, Dhaka, Karachi and Algiers also near the bottom of the index - cities affected by conflict or instability.
  3. Good exam practice - pick one liveability factor (e.g. access to services, environmental quality, community/social connectedness) and compare how it plays out in Gosford versus one highly-ranked and one low-ranked city above.
  4. Worth remembering too - a ranking like this only measures what it chooses to measure (the EIU uses 30 indicators). It says little about belonging, culture or spiritual wellbeing - good for a "how reliable is this data?" question (Ch.6.4-6.5).

Revision Ideas

Influences and perceptions (Ch.6)

  • Environmental factors: climate, green space, pollution, natural hazards.
  • Human/social factors: safety, culture, community, income, services.
  • Two people can rate the same place very differently - liveability is partly personal.
  • Measuring liveability mixes quantitative data (crime rate, income) and qualitative data (survey opinions).
Mrs Perkins - from the iLearn Place and Liveability lessons

Access to services and facilities (Ch.7)

  • Key services: healthcare, education, transport, emergency services, green space.
  • Access depends on location - rural areas often travel much further for the same service than urban areas.
  • Limited access lowers liveability even when other factors (climate, safety) are strong.
Mrs Perkins - from the iLearn Place and Liveability lessons

Environmental quality (Ch.8)

  • Reduced by pollution, land degradation, natural hazards and urban sprawl.
  • Case study: Mount Atkinson Estate, Truganina VIC, and the proposed Werribee satellite city, built to ease sprawl around growing outer-Melbourne suburbs.
  • Traffic congestion case study: Melbourne, where 83.9% of households own at least one car, compared with Malmo, Sweden (420km of cycle paths, a quarter of trips by bike).
  • Cities produce around three-quarters of the world's carbon dioxide emissions.
Threats to Liveability (class resource)

Community (Ch.9)

  • Social connectedness is built through shared spaces, local groups and feeling safe.
  • Perceived safety (how safe people feel) isn't always the same as actual safety (crime statistics).
  • Safer design: clear sightlines, buildings facing public spaces, natural surveillance (people can see the space) and formal surveillance (cameras, patrols).
  • Neighbourhood Watch enhances connectedness directly: know your neighbours, share information, build trust.
Liveability in Australia: Building Safe Communities (class resource)

Enhancing liveability (Ch.10)

  • Different groups play different roles: government (planning, infrastructure), NGOs (300 Blankets - supporting people experiencing homelessness), community groups, and individuals.
  • Planning strategy to know: the 15-Minute City - designing neighbourhoods so daily needs are a short walk, cycle or ride away.
  • Caring for Country: Aboriginal joint management of land across NSW improves liveability and environmental quality together.
