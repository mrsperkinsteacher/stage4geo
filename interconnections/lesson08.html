📋 Summary of Lesson

A transport network is made of nodes (points such as airports, ports and stations) and links (the routes between them). A busy node where many links meet is called a hub. Today you learn this vocabulary, use airport passenger data to find the maximum, minimum, range and rank, and describe the pattern a network makes across space.

This matters because networks decide which places are easy to reach and which are not. Once you can describe a network in these terms, you can ask who it works well for - the question for the next lesson.

📖 Glossary

Network - a system of connected places. A transport network moves people and goods between them.
Node - a point in a network where routes meet - for example, a port, airport or railway station.
Link - a route that connects two nodes - for example, a shipping route, flight path, road or railway.
Hub - a busy node where many links meet, such as a major international airport.
Spatial pattern - the way things are arranged across space - dense or sparse, connected or isolated.

💡 Did You Know?

On one day in August 2013, 2,524 flights crossed the North Atlantic, and 1,273 of them passed through the Shanwick Oceanic Control Area, which the UK's NATS and the Irish Aviation Authority manage together. That is what a busy air link looks like.

Source: NATS, 'North Atlantic Skies' (2014), video description on Vimeo.

📰 In the News

Western Sydney International Airport opens to passengers on Sunday 25 October 2026, with freight flights starting on 26 July 2026. It is designed to serve up to 10 million passengers a year, will run without a curfew, and the first passenger flight is a Jetstar service to the Gold Coast. A new node means new links across the network.

🗺️ Where in the World?

Sydney - in 2024 more than 41 million passengers passed through Sydney Airport's terminals: 16.3 million international and more than 25 million domestic. From launch, the new Western Sydney airport adds links to Melbourne, the Gold Coast and Brisbane, with Auckland from 26 October 2026 and Singapore from 23 November 2026.

Sources: Sydney Airport, Annual Traffic Performance Q4 2024; Prime Minister of Australia, 10 June 2026.

💼 Careers Connected to This Lesson

Air traffic controller - guides aircraft safely along the links between airports, like the controllers who manage the North Atlantic flights in the video.

Transport planner - studies where people and goods need to travel and designs the nodes and links, such as new rail lines, roads and airports, that make it possible.
