📋 Summary of Lesson

Today you compare flying in the 1950s with flying now, and meet the idea of time-space convergence: the world hasn't changed size, but it feels smaller because faster, cheaper transport means places take less time to reach. Then you build a line graph of Australian overseas departures and describe the trend using real numbers from your graph.

This matters because geographers use graphs to spot patterns. The big dip in the data around 2020 is a reminder that a trend can change suddenly when something major happens. (The worksheet uses rounded sample data based on Australian Bureau of Statistics trends.)

📖 Glossary

Time-space convergence - the idea that the world 'feels smaller' because faster, cheaper transport means places take less time to reach.
Departure - a trip out of a country. 'Overseas departures' counts people leaving Australia to travel abroad.
Trend - the overall direction of data over time - rising, falling or staying steady.
Technology - tools, machines and systems that people invent to solve problems - for example, the jet engine.
Line graph - a graph that joins plotted points with straight lines to show how something changes over time.

📰 In the News

In September 2026 the Australian Bureau of Statistics reported 1,095,800 short-term resident departures for July 2026 - Australians leaving the country for a trip abroad. The ABS counts border crossings, not people, so one person who travels twice is counted twice. This is the same kind of data you graph in this lesson.

Source: Australian Bureau of Statistics, Overseas Arrivals and Departures, Australia, July 2026 (released 11 September 2026).

🗺️ Where in the World?

Sydney to London - about 17,000 km apart. Qantas plans to fly it non-stop from October 2027 in specially modified Airbus A350-1000 aircraft carrying an extra fuel tank, in about 22 hours. Compare that with the days of hopping between refuelling stops that the same journey once took.

Source: Executive Traveller, 17 June 2026.

💼 Careers Connected to This Lesson

Airline pilot - flies passengers and freight between airports. Pilots need to understand weather, navigation and how air routes link places together.

Data analyst - collects numbers such as travel and tourism statistics, graphs them and explains the trend. It is exactly the skill you practise in the line graph task.
