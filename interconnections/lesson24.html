Labels, boycotts and scorecards all promise to fix palm oil. Find out how to judge which ones actually work.

📋 Summary of Lesson

Today you look at three strategies for managing palm oil: certification (the RSPO label), boycotts and scorecards. You find out how each one is meant to work, check real data from a study in Indonesia, and write a short judgement backed by evidence.

This matters because a promise is not proof. To evaluate a strategy you need evidence, and you need to notice who is speaking. WWF, the IUCN and the RSPO each look at palm oil from a different point of view.

📖 Glossary

Certification - a label showing a product meets a set of standards, checked by someone outside the company.
Evaluate - to judge how well something works, using evidence.
Boycott - refusing to buy something as a protest or to force change.
Evidence - facts or data that help show whether something is true.
Trade-off - giving up one good thing to gain another.

💡 Did You Know?

The RSPO website shows about 5.7 million hectares of certified oil palm land and more than 8,000 facilities with supply chain certificates. These are the RSPO's own figures.

Source: RSPO website, rspo.org, checked October 2026.

📰 In the News

In September 2026 the RSPO's own impact report said about 16.2 million tonnes of certified palm oil were sold in 2025, about 20 per cent of world supply. It said certified supply could reach nearly 40 per cent. Remember who is speaking: the RSPO promotes its own scheme.

Source: RSPO Impact Report 2026, published on Eco-Business, 28 September 2026.

🗺️ Where in the World?

Latin America and Africa - palm oil is not only grown in Southeast Asia. The RSPO says its members now hold nearly 75 per cent of Latin America's palm oil output, and certified supply from Africa rose by about 46 per cent.

Source: RSPO Impact Report 2026, via Eco-Business.
