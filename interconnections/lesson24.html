Labels, safety agreements and brand report cards all promise a fairer t-shirt. Find out how to judge which ones actually work.

📋 Summary of Lesson

Today you test three real strategies for managing the cotton t-shirt: Fairtrade cotton (a certification label), the International Accord (a legally binding safety agreement for garment factories) and the Ethical Fashion Report (a guide that grades fashion brands). You look at what each one does, the evidence behind it and its catch, then compare that evidence with a peer-reviewed study of palm oil certification.

This matters because a promise is not proof. To evaluate a strategy you need evidence, and you need to notice who is speaking. Fairtrade, the Accord and Baptist World Aid each report on their own work, so you ask four questions about any evidence: Who? Compared with what? How big? When?

📖 Glossary

Certification - a label showing a product meets a set of standards, checked by someone outside the company.
Evaluate - to judge how well something works, using evidence.
Evidence - facts or data that help show whether something is true.
Legally binding - an agreement that can be enforced, so it is more than a promise.
Living wage - pay that is enough for a worker and their family to afford the basics, such as food and housing.

💡 Did You Know?

In September 2026, 300 garment brands and retailers had signed the International Accord, the highest number for any Accord agreement so far. Together they buy from more than 2,000 factories in Bangladesh and Pakistan.

Source: International Accord, 9 September 2026.

📰 In the News

The International Accord was renewed in 2023 for three years, with automatic renewal for another three. In September 2026 it reported its highest number of signatories, 13 years after the Rana Plaza factory collapse in Bangladesh that led to the first Accord.

Source: International Accord, 9 September 2026.

🗺️ Where in the World?

Dhaka, Bangladesh - on 24 April 2013 the Rana Plaza building, which held garment factories, collapsed and killed at least 1,134 people. It is the reason the Accord exists, and why Bangladesh is at the centre of today's lesson.

Source: International Labour Organization, Bangladesh, Rana Plaza and beyond.
