Lesson 25: Walking in Their Shoes

One cheap t-shirt, five people, five very different views. Step into someone else's shoes and hear every side before you decide.

📋 Summary of Lesson

Today you take on one of five roles: a garment worker in Dhaka, a fashion company executive, a trade minister, a campaigner from a non-government organisation (NGO) and a teenage shopper. You prepare what your stakeholder wants and fears, speak in role, and listen to the other four. Your group tries to agree on one change all five could accept, then you step out of role for a debrief.

This matters because real decisions about trade are not made from facts alone. A fact can be checked and is true for everyone. A perspective is shaped by a person's situation, values and experience. Hearing every side first is the preparation for the next lesson, where you make your own judgement. If a group cannot agree, that is a finding, not a failure.

📖 Glossary

Stakeholder - a person or group affected by a decision, or able to affect it.
Perspective - a way of seeing an issue, shaped by your situation, values and experiences.
Management - actions taken to change or protect something, such as how fast fashion is run and regulated.
Living wage - pay that covers the real cost of living: food, housing, health care and schooling.
Audit - an independent check, for example of safety and working conditions in a factory.

🌍 Link to the SDGs

SDG 8 - Decent Work and Economic Growth

Target 8.8 is about protecting workers' rights and safe working conditions, which is what the garment worker wants most.
SDG 12 - Responsible Consumption and Production

Target 12.6 encourages large companies to adopt sustainable practices and report on them, which is what the campaigner asks of the executive.
SDG 17 - Partnerships for the Goals

Target 17.17 promotes partnerships between governments, companies and community groups: the kind of deal the roleplay is looking for.

💡 Did You Know?

Bangladesh's garment industry employs more than 4.4 million people, about 70 per cent of them women, and earns over 80 per cent of the country's export income. In December 2023 the minimum wage for garment workers rose from 8,000 to 12,500 taka a month (about US$114). Unions had asked for 23,000 taka.

Source: FashionUnited, 'What the new minimum wage means for garment workers in Bangladesh', 8 November 2023.

📰 In the News

Bangladesh was due to leave the United Nations list of Least Developed Countries on 24 November 2026. That status helps its garments enter markets such as the European Union and Australia without duties, so leaving it could make its clothes dearer to sell overseas. In February 2026 Bangladesh asked for three more years to prepare. In June 2026 a UN committee supported moving the date to 24 November 2029, with the final say resting with the UN General Assembly. It is a real example of a trade minister balancing jobs, income and the future.

Sources: The Financial Express (Dhaka), 20 February 2026; The Daily Star (Dhaka), 2 June 2026 and 28 June 2024; DFAT, Bangladesh country brief.

🗺️ Where in the World?

Australia and Bangladesh - the two countries trade in both directions. DFAT says two-way trade is worth about $5 billion, and Australia's exports to Bangladesh reached $3.3 billion in 2024, making it Australia's 22nd largest export market. Australia's imports from Bangladesh include ready-made garments, which is why the teenage shopper in today's roleplay is part of the same story as the worker in Dhaka.

Source: DFAT, Bangladesh country brief, checked October 2026.

💼 Careers Connected to This Lesson

Trade union organiser - speaks up for workers in talks with factory owners and brands about pay, hours and safety, like the worker's voice in the roleplay.

Trade negotiator or diplomat - works for a government department such as DFAT, representing a country's interests in trade talks while listening to what other countries need.
