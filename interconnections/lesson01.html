📋 Summary of Lesson

Today you're starting to notice something that's usually invisible: how connected your everyday life already is to people and places all over the world. You'll look at ordinary things - your breakfast, your phone, your clothes - and trace where they really come from. Then you'll build a class definition of interconnection and draw your own sketch map showing the personal connections in your life, from family and food to overseas places.

This matters because the rest of the term builds on this idea - that nothing in your life exists in isolation. It's all part of a bigger, connected world.

📖 Glossary

Interconnection - the relationships between people, places and environments.
Trade - buying, selling or swapping goods and services between people, businesses or countries.
Globalisation - the way the world's people, businesses and cultures are becoming more connected.
Sketch map - a simple hand-drawn map showing the main features of a place, not drawn to exact scale.
BOLTSS - the six things every good map needs: Border, Orientation, Legend, Title, Scale, Source.

💡 Did You Know?

Nike doesn't make its shoes in just one country - its footwear alone is produced across roughly 11 countries, and its full supply chain (materials, components, assembly) spans 37+ countries in total.

Source: Cosmo Sourcing industry blog, citing Nike's public supply-chain disclosures.

📰 In the News

28 March 2024: China officially lifted tariffs of up to 218% on Australian wine, ending a three-year trade freeze that began in 2021 - a real, recent example of how trade relationships between countries can change quickly.

Source: ABC News, 28 March 2024, "China's government officially abolishes heavy tariffs on Australian wine."

2026: New United States tariffs of between 10% and 50% now apply to around $20 billion of Australia's yearly exports to the US, hitting the metals and advanced manufacturing industries hardest - a live example of how a single policy decision overseas can ripple straight through Australian trade.

Source: Australian Industry Group, "Trade, Tariffs and Supply Chains" research briefing, 2026.

26 May 2026: Australia, India, Japan and the United States launched the Quad Critical Minerals Initiative Framework, a joint agreement to secure and diversify the supply chains behind everyday technology - proof that countries are actively building new global partnerships, not just reacting to old ones.

Source: Quad Critical Minerals Initiative Framework, announced 26 May 2026.

🗺️ Where in the World?

Dhaka, Bangladesh - Bangladesh is the world's second-largest clothing exporter (behind only China), and its garment industry employs around four million workers. This is the real place behind the idea that "a factory worker in Bangladesh may have sewn your school bag."

Ho Chi Minh City area, Vietnam Added 20/09 - Vietnam is now Nike's single biggest manufacturing base worldwide, producing 51% of all Nike footwear in FY2025 - more than any other country, including China.

Shenzhen, Guangdong, China Added 20/09 - a manufacturing megacity nicknamed the "Silicon Valley of Hardware," home to huge electronics factories (including major Foxconn plants) that assemble much of the world's phones, tablets and other consumer tech.

💼 Careers Connected to This Lesson

Supply chain analyst - tracks how a product like a sneaker or phone moves from raw materials to your hands, mapping and improving every step of the kind of 37+ country supply chain covered in today's Did You Know box.

International trade economist - studies how tariffs, trade deals and exchange rates affect what a country buys and sells, the exact issues behind the wine tariffs and minerals agreements in today's In the News box.

🎓 What You'll Study in Stage 5 & 6

Keep pulling this thread and here's where it leads. The idea you started today - that nothing in your life exists in isolation - becomes the foundation of two entire senior subjects.

Stage 5 - Human Wellbeing: zooms out from your own sneakers and phone to compare quality of life across entire countries, asking why the global connections and inequalities you noticed today leave some places thriving and others struggling.

Stage 6 (HSC) - People and Economic Activity / Global Challenges: goes even further, using real economic data to map how trade, production and consumption connect the whole planet - the exact foundation laid in this lesson, but with the tools to measure and explain it.
