📋 Summary of Lesson

Today you write the most important paragraph of the unit. You learn the difference between describing, explaining and judging, recap the four strategies for managing fast fashion (consumer choices, company action, government laws and certification), plan your answer, then write one paragraph that picks a strategy, backs it with evidence from the unit and weighs up an alternative.

This matters because there is no single right strategy. What counts is the quality of your reasons and evidence, and whether you are fair to the other side. That is how real decisions about trade get made, and it is a skill you will use in every subject: take a position, defend it, and admit its limits.

📖 Glossary

Evaluate - to make a judgement about value or importance, backed by reasons and evidence.
Strategy - a planned approach to managing a problem, such as laws or certification.
Certification - an independent label showing a product meets set standards, such as fair pay.
Code of conduct - a company's own rules for how its suppliers must treat workers and the environment.
Evidence - facts, examples or data used to support a claim.

🌍 Link to the SDGs

SDG 12 - Responsible Consumption and Production

Target 12.6 asks large companies to adopt sustainable practices and report on them, one of the strategies you weigh today.
SDG 8 - Decent Work and Economic Growth

💡 Did You Know?

Baptist World Aid's 2024 Ethical Fashion Report looked at 120 companies earning more than $50 million in Australia, covering 460 brands. It found that 89 per cent were not paying a fair wage at any stage of their supply chains.

Source: Business and Human Rights Resource Centre, 'Australia: Report concludes brands failing to pay garment workers a living wage', 17 October 2024.

📰 In the News

On 1 September 2026 France began charging a fee on each item sold by large ultra-fast fashion platforms. In 2026 it is about 2 euros for a t-shirt and 12 euros for a jacket, rising to as much as 19.50 euros per item by 2030. The fee can never be more than half the item's price before tax. It is a government strategy in action, the same idea BTN reported on in 2024.

Source: Euronews, 1 September 2026.

🗺️ Where in the World?

Xinjiang, China and the United States - since June 2022, a United States law treats goods made wholly or partly in China's Xinjiang region as made with forced labour. They cannot enter the United States unless the importer proves otherwise with clear and convincing evidence. Because it covers goods made 'in part' there, clothing made with Xinjiang cotton is included. It shows a government law working at the border, and also its limit: it only applies to goods entering that one country.

Source: U.S. Customs and Border Protection, Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act, checked October 2026.
