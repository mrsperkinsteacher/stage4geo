Lesson 16: Same Trade, Different Outcome

A banana can cost more one week than the next. Money, government decisions and the natural world all change what we trade and what it costs.

📋 Summary of Lesson

Today you sort the things that change trade into three kinds of influence: economic (money and costs), political (government decisions such as tariffs, trade agreements and sanctions) and environmental (the natural world, such as cyclones and droughts). You start with a real case, Cyclone Yasi and the price of bananas, and then build cause, effect and outcome chains.

This matters because the same product can have a very different journey and price depending on what is happening in the world. A storm in Queensland, a tax on imported cars or a new deal between countries can change what ends up in your shopping trolley.

📖 Glossary

Influence - something that changes what is traded, where it goes or what it costs.
Supply and demand - supply is how much of something is for sale; demand is how much people want to buy it.
Exchange rate - what one country's money is worth compared with another country's money.
Tariff - a tax a government puts on imported goods, making them more expensive.
Trade agreement - a deal between countries to make trade easier, usually by cutting tariffs.
Sanction - a government rule that bans or limits trade with another country.

💡 Did You Know?

In 2024 two big economies put extra charges on electric cars made in China. The United States set a tariff of 100 per cent from 27 September 2024. The European Union set extra duties of between 7.8 and 35.3 per cent from 30 October 2024, depending on the carmaker.

Sources: European Commission, Access2Markets, 2024; E&E News, 16 September 2024.

📰 In the News

In March 2026 Australia and the European Union finalised a free trade agreement after eight years of talks. DFAT says 97.8 per cent of the current value of Australia's exports to the EU will enter duty free from day one, rising to 98 per cent. The deal starts only once both sides finish their own approval steps.

Sources: DFAT, Australia-EU Free Trade Agreement highlights, March 2026; Process Online, 25 March 2026.

🗺️ Where in the World?

Far North Queensland - Severe Tropical Cyclone Yasi crossed the coast near Mission Beach between midnight and 1 am on 3 February 2011, then tracked west across northern Queensland. Banana crops in the region were badly damaged, and by 4 February Woolworths had raised its shelf price to $5.98 a kilo.

Sources: Bureau of Meteorology, Monthly weather review Queensland, February 2011; Food Processing, 4 February 2011.
