Shoppers, companies and governments can all change the t-shirt trade. Find out what each one can do, and where each one runs out of power.

📋 Summary of Lesson

Today you look at real strategies for making the t-shirt trade fairer and more sustainable. You sort them into three levels: what consumers, companies and governments can do. Then you rank them, give reasons, and choose one realistic change you could make yourself.

This matters because each level has different powers and limits. A shopper can choose but often lacks information. A company controls its own suppliers but many schemes are voluntary. A law can reach every big business, but only inside one country.

📖 Glossary

Ethical consumption - choosing what to buy by how it affects people and the environment, not only by price.
Audit - an independent check of a factory to see whether workers are treated fairly and safely.
Certification - a label showing that a product meets a set of standards, checked by someone outside the company.
Transparency - when a company openly shares information, such as which factories make its clothes.
Modern slavery - when people are forced to work through threats, debt or deception, and cannot leave.

💡 Did You Know?

Baptist World Aid's 2024 Ethical Fashion Report assessed 120 companies selling clothes in Australia. It found 89 per cent were not paying a living wage at any stage of their supply chains.

Source: Sydney Morning Herald, reprinted by the Business and Human Rights Resource Centre, 17 October 2024.

📰 In the News

Since 2018, Australian businesses with at least A$100 million in yearly revenue must publish a modern slavery statement on a public register. On 16 July 2026 the Federal Government said it plans to add civil penalties and a new criminal offence for big companies that fail to prevent modern slavery in their supply chains. The details are still to come and it is not yet law.

Sources: Parliament of Australia, Bills Digest, Modern Slavery Bill 2018; Baker McKenzie, 20 July 2026.

🗺️ Where in the World?

Bangladesh and Pakistan - after the Rana Plaza collapse, over 40 companies and a group of unions signed a safety Accord on 15 May 2013. Engineers inspected over 1,000 factories in its first year. It became the International Accord in 2021, and a Pakistan Accord began on 1 January 2023.

Sources: International Accord, About us.

💼 Careers Connected to This Lesson

Ethical sourcing manager - works for a clothing brand, checks its factories and suppliers, and fixes problems with safety or wages.

Policy adviser - helps a government design laws such as the Modern Slavery Act and checks whether they are working.
