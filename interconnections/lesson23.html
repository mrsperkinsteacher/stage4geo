Lesson 23: The Palm Oil Problem

Palm oil is in food, soap and shampoo. Find out where it comes from, what it costs forests, and why banning it is not simple.

📋 Summary of Lesson

Today you find out what palm oil is and where it is produced. You build a pie chart from real production data, learn the other names palm oil can hide behind on a label, and weigh up the harm it can do to forests against the living it gives farmers.

This matters because the same crop can cause harm and give a living. Oil palm grows a lot of oil on a small area of land, but clearing forest and draining peat for plantations harms animals, people and the climate. Managing it means weighing both sides.

📖 Glossary

Plantation - a large farm where one crop, such as oil palm, is grown in rows.
Deforestation - clearing forest so the land can be used for something else.
Peatland - wet land full of partly rotted plants that stores a lot of carbon.
Smallholder - a farmer who grows crops on a small area of land, often a family farm.
Yield - how much of a crop or product you get from a set area of land.

💡 Did You Know?

In 2017 palm oil made up about 36 per cent of the world's vegetable oil, but used only about 8.6 per cent of the land used for oil crops. The IUCN gives almost the same picture: 35 per cent of the oil on under 10 per cent of the land.

Sources: Our World in Data, Palm oil (Hannah Ritchie); IUCN press release, 26 June 2018.

📰 In the News

The European Union has a law to stop products linked to deforestation, including palm oil, being sold in the EU. It has been delayed twice. In December 2025 EU lawmakers agreed it will apply to large companies from 30 December 2026 and to smaller ones from 30 June 2027. Palm oil groups in Malaysia and Indonesia welcomed the earlier delay, and Indonesian growers say smallholders must not be shut out.

Sources: FoodNavigator, 8 October 2024; ConfectioneryNews, 5 December 2025.

🗺️ Where in the World?

Southeast Asia - when tropical peat forests are drained for plantations, they release carbon, and peat fires are hard to put out. Smoke from these fires harms people's health across the region.

Sources: WWF Australia, Palm oil.
