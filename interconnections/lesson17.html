One ship stuck in one canal for six days. Find out how the delay spread to factories, shops and homes around the world.

📋 Summary of Lesson

Today you look at what happened when the container ship Ever Given ran aground in the Suez Canal, Egypt, on 23 March 2021. You study a satellite photo, follow how the shock spread step by step, then take on the role of one imagined person affected by the blockage. As a class you sort the impacts on producers, consumers and environments, in the short term and the long term.

This matters because about 12 per cent of world trade normally passes through the canal. When one narrow point on a trade route is blocked, the delay travels along the links to people who never see the canal. That is interconnection in action.

📖 Glossary

Trade shock - a sudden event that interrupts or changes trade flows.
Supply chain - all the steps and places a product passes through between producer and consumer.
Freight - goods moved in bulk by ship, plane, train or truck, and the cost of moving them.
Reroute - to send ships or goods by a different, usually longer, path.
Interconnection - the way places, people and economies are linked and depend on one another.
Trade flow - the movement of goods and services from one place to another.

💡 Did You Know?

About 19,000 ships used the Suez Canal in 2020, roughly 50 a day. More than 1 billion tonnes of cargo went through it in 2019, about four times as much as the Panama Canal.

Source: New Zealand Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade, The importance of the Suez Canal to global trade, 18 April 2021.

📰 In the News

The Suez route was disrupted again from late 2023 during the Red Sea crisis. By early March 2024, Suez Canal transits were down 42 per cent from their 2023 peak. Many ships went around Africa instead, which added about 12 days to a voyage from Shanghai to Rotterdam.

Source: UNCTAD, 'Red Sea crisis and implications for trade facilitation in Africa', 17 April 2024.

🗺️ Where in the World?

Cape of Good Hope, South Africa - the southern tip of Africa is the long way round when ships cannot use the Suez Canal. The detour adds about 9,000 kilometres. In early 2024 so many container ships came this way that ports in Durban and Cape Town became congested.

Sources: NZ Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade (2021); UNCTAD (2024).

💼 Careers Connected to This Lesson

Marine pilot - an expert who boards large ships and guides them through canals, rivers and busy ports, where one mistake can block the way for everyone.

Supply chain manager - plans how goods move from factories to shops, and finds back-up routes and suppliers when a trade shock hits.
