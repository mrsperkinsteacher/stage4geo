📋 Summary of Lesson

Today you define the digital divide - the gap between people who have good access to technology and the internet, and people who do not. You describe it using data about internet access in different places, look at who it affects most (including people in remote places and some First Nations communities), and finish with a four-part written response that includes causes and strategies.

This matters because access means more than owning a phone. It needs a suitable device, a reliable connection, the skills to use it and the money to afford it. Where those are missing, people can miss out on school, health care, jobs and information.

📖 Glossary

Digital divide - the gap between people who have good access to digital technology and the internet, and people who do not.
Access - having a suitable device, a reliable connection, the skills to use it, and the money to afford it.
Remote - far away from cities and large towns, where services are harder and more expensive to provide.
Telehealth - seeing a doctor or health worker by video or phone instead of travelling to a clinic.
Strategy - a planned action designed to solve or reduce a problem.

💡 Did You Know?

In 2025 about 85 per cent of people in cities were online, compared with about 58 per cent of people in rural areas. Globally, about 6 billion people are online, and about 2.2 billion - roughly 1 in 4 - are not.

Source: International Telecommunication Union (ITU), Facts and Figures 2025, 17 November 2025. The 'about 1 in 4' share is worked out from the ITU's 6 billion online and 2.2 billion offline.

📰 In the News

The 2025 Australian Digital Inclusion Index found that digital inclusion is improving: between 2023 and 2025 the national Access score rose from 72.0 to 76.8, and Digital Ability rose from 64.9 to 73.6. But gaps remain. The gap between First Nations people and other Australians is 10.5 points nationally, 16.5 points in remote areas and 22.8 points in very remote areas.

Source: Australian Digital Inclusion Index, 'The 2025 findings', published late 2025.

🗺️ Where in the World?

Remote and very remote Australia - distance and cost combine to make good internet harder to get. The 2025 index found the First Nations digital inclusion gap widens from 10.5 points nationally to 16.5 points in remote areas and 22.8 points in very remote areas, and closing it is a national priority under Closing the Gap Target 17.

Sources: Australian Digital Inclusion Index 2025; Productivity Commission, Closing the Gap dashboard.

💼 Careers Connected to This Lesson

Telehealth nurse or doctor - cares for patients by video or phone, which only works when the patient has a reliable connection.

Community digital inclusion worker - helps people in libraries and community centres get online, learn digital skills and stay safe on the internet.
