Somebody made the shirt you are wearing. Meet the people behind our clothes and the question that is changing fashion.

📋 Summary of Lesson

Today you think about the people who make our clothes. You watch a short experiment from Berlin, learn what happened at Rana Plaza in Bangladesh in 2013, and look at why a t-shirt can cost so little. Then you decide who you think is most responsible for how garment workers are treated, and write respectful questions you would ask a garment worker. The lesson is factual and calm, and it uses no photographs of Rana Plaza.

This matters because the price of a t-shirt does not show its whole cost. Some of that cost is carried by the people who make it. Information and campaigns can change what shoppers and brands do.

📖 Glossary

Fast fashion - cheap clothing made quickly to follow the latest trends.
Garment worker - a person employed to cut, sew and finish clothing.
Sociocultural impact - an effect on people, communities and their ways of life.
Transparency - when a brand openly shares where and by whom its clothes are made.
Rana Plaza - a building in Savar, near Dhaka, Bangladesh, that held garment factories and collapsed on 24 April 2013.

🌍 Link to the SDGs

SDG 8 - Decent Work and Economic Growth

Goal 8 includes safe and secure working environments for all workers. Rana Plaza showed why building safety matters.
SDG 12 - Responsible Consumption and Production

Responsible consumption means thinking about how clothes are made, buying less and keeping them longer.
SDG 5 - Gender Equality

Most of the people killed at Rana Plaza were young women. Safe, fair work for women is part of gender equality.

💡 Did You Know?

In one Clean Clothes Campaign estimate for a 29 Euro t-shirt made in Bangladesh and sold in Europe, almost 60 per cent of the price goes to the retailer and 12 per cent is brand profit. The factory workers receive less than 1 per cent.

Source: Statista (Felix Richter), 22 May 2025, using Clean Clothes Campaign estimates. This is an estimate for one example.

📰 In the News

In April 2026 Fashion Revolution closed its UK office because of a hard funding climate. The movement it started after Rana Plaza carries on through its network of country teams, and Fashion Revolution Week is still held each year around 24 April.

Sources: FashionUnited, 10 April 2026; Fashion Revolution, About.

🗺️ Where in the World?

Savar, Bangladesh - a town near the capital, Dhaka, where the eight-storey Rana Plaza building stood. It held several garment factories. When it collapsed on 24 April 2013, more than 1,100 people died. The date is now remembered around the world each year.

Sources: Business and Human Rights Resource Centre; Fashion Revolution.
