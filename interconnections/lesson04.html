📋 Summary of Lesson

This lesson is about what "Country" really means to Aboriginal Peoples - not just land, but identity, law, responsibility and economy all connected together. You'll unpack an Acknowledgement of Country line by line, learn the difference between an Acknowledgement and a Welcome to Country, and use the real AIATSIS map to find out whose Country your own school stands on.

It matters because this connection to Country isn't something from the past - Traditional Custodians care for Country today through things like ranger programs, cultural burning and First Nations businesses.

📖 Glossary

Country - the lands, waters and skies an Aboriginal People belongs to, including living things, stories, law and responsibilities. Written with a capital C.
Acknowledgement of Country - a statement of respect, which anyone can give, recognising the Traditional Custodians of a place.
Welcome to Country - a ceremony that can only be given by a Traditional Custodian of that Country.
Traditional Custodians - the Aboriginal People who hold ongoing responsibility to care for a particular Country.
Elders - people recognised by their community as respected holders of knowledge, culture and lore.
Custodianship - the responsibility to care for something so it can be passed on to future generations.

💡 Did You Know?

📰 In the News

25 April 2025 (Anzac Day): Welcome to Country ceremonies at dawn services in Sydney, Melbourne and Perth were interrupted by booing from parts of the crowd, drawing condemnation from Indigenous leaders, politicians and military figures, and reigniting public debate about Welcome to Country.

Source: ABC News, 25 April 2025.

27 May - 3 June 2026: Australia marked National Reconciliation Week 2026 under the theme "All In," calling on every Australian - in schools, workplaces and communities - to take an active part in reconciliation, including through Acknowledgement of Country.

Source: Reconciliation Australia, National Reconciliation Week 2026.

2026: NSW public schools continue to be guided by the NSW Department of Education's official protocol on Acknowledgement of and Welcome to Country, which sets out when each should be used and by whom - the exact distinction covered in this lesson.

Source: NSW Department of Education, "Welcome to Country and Acknowledgement of Country" protocol document.

🗺️ Where in the World?

This lesson deliberately doesn't name one specific Country - you'll find your own school's using the AIATSIS map, since the answer is different for every school. As a worked example: much of the Sydney CBD sits on Gadigal Country, part of the Eora Nation.

Melbourne CBD, VIC Added 20/09 - a second worked example: Melbourne's CBD sits on Wurundjeri Country, part of the Kulin Nation, whose peoples have maintained an unbroken connection to the land along the Birrarung (Yarra River) for more than 2,000 generations.

Brisbane CBD, QLD Added 20/09 - a third worked example: Brisbane's CBD (Meanjin) sits on the Country of the Turrbal and Yuggera peoples, who have lived in the area for tens of thousands of years.

🎓 What You'll Study in Stage 5 & 6

Caring for Country is not just history - it is a live case study in senior Geography.

Stage 5 - Environmental Change and Management and Sustainable Biomes: both regularly use Aboriginal land management approaches, including cultural burning and Caring for Country programs, as real case studies of environments being managed well.

Stage 6 (HSC) - Ecosystems at Risk: makes Traditional Owner land management strategies a genuine, frequently examined case study for protecting ecosystems under threat - built directly on the concept of Country you learned today.
