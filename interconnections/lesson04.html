📋 Summary of Lesson

This lesson is about what "Country" really means to Aboriginal Peoples - not just land, but identity, law, responsibility and economy all connected together. You'll unpack an Acknowledgement of Country line by line, learn the difference between an Acknowledgement and a Welcome to Country, and use the real AIATSIS map to find out whose Country your own school stands on.

It matters because this connection to Country isn't something from the past - Traditional Custodians care for Country today through things like ranger programs, cultural burning and First Nations businesses.

📖 Glossary

Country - the lands, waters and skies an Aboriginal People belongs to, including living things, stories, law and responsibilities. Written with a capital C.
Acknowledgement of Country - a statement of respect, which anyone can give, recognising the Traditional Custodians of a place.
Welcome to Country - a ceremony that can only be given by a Traditional Custodian of that Country.
Traditional Custodians - the Aboriginal People who hold ongoing responsibility to care for a particular Country.
Elders - people recognised by their community as respected holders of knowledge, culture and lore.
Custodianship - the responsibility to care for something so it can be passed on to future generations.

🌍 Link to the SDGs

  • SDG 15 - Life on Land: Country includes the responsibility to care for land, waters and living things.
  • SDG 16 - Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions: naming whose Country we're on is part of acting justly.
  • SDG 4 - Quality Education (Target 4.7): calls for education that builds appreciation of cultural diversity - exactly what an Acknowledgement of Country aims to build.

💡 Did You Know?

At the time of British colonisation, there were more than 250 distinct Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander languages spoken across the continent - each tied to its own Country.

Source: AIATSIS.

📰 In the News

On 25 April 2025 (Anzac Day), Welcome to Country ceremonies at dawn services in Sydney, Melbourne and Perth were interrupted by booing from parts of the crowd, drawing condemnation from Indigenous leaders, politicians and military figures, and reigniting public debate about Welcome to Country.

Source: ABC News, 25 April 2025.

🗺️ Where in the World?

This lesson deliberately doesn't name one specific Country - you'll find your own school's using the AIATSIS map, since the answer is different for every school. As a worked example: much of the Sydney CBD sits on Gadigal Country, part of the Eora Nation.
