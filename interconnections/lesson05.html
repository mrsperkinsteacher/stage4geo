📋 Summary of Lesson

Long before ships arrived in 1788, First Nations Peoples were already trading across the whole continent - ochre from Western Australia, pearl shell from the Kimberley, and stone axes from near Melbourne all travelled thousands of kilometres along routes guided by songlines. This lesson looks at that ancient trade network, then compares it with First Nations economic activity happening right now - some highly visible (like art sold in galleries worldwide), some "unseen" by most Australians (like rangers caring for Country).

📖 Glossary

Trade network - a web of routes and relationships along which goods, ideas and knowledge are exchanged.
Songline - a route across Country carrying song, story, law and knowledge; songlines also guided travel and trade.
Ochre - a natural earth pigment used for art, ceremony and trade.
Economy - the way a community produces, exchanges and shares the things people need and value.
Cultural tourism - tourism where visitors pay to learn about culture from the people who own that culture.
Country - a living, interconnected relationship with land, waters and community, not just a piece of territory.
Native Title - legal recognition of First Nations Peoples' rights and interests in land and waters.

🌍 Link to the SDGs

  • SDG 8 - Decent Work and Economic Growth: art centres, cultural tourism, ranger jobs and First Nations-owned companies connect local communities to national and global markets.
  • SDG 15 - Life on Land: Indigenous ranger programs support conservation of tens of millions of hectares.
  • SDG 16 - Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions: recognition of First Nations economies ties to land rights, Native Title and fair treatment.

💡 Did You Know?

Archaeological research has found evidence that marine shell was carried at least 200km inland roughly 3,200 years ago - showing how far and how long Aboriginal trade networks connected with Papua New Guinea and Torres Strait societies.

Source: The Conversation, August 2025, on the "Coral Sea Cultural Interaction Sphere" research.

📰 In the News

In May 2026, the Federal Court delivered Australia's largest-ever native title compensation award: roughly $150 million to the Yindjibarndi people of the Pilbara (WA) for cultural loss caused by a mining operation that made 240 sacred sites inaccessible.

Source: ABC News, 13 May 2026; National Indigenous Times, July 2026.

🗺️ Where in the World?

Arnhem Land, Northern Territory - from at least the early 1700s, decades before British colonisation, Makassan fishermen from Sulawesi sailed to the Arnhem Land coast each monsoon season to trade with Yolngu people for trepang, exchanging metal tools, cloth and even loan-words still used today.
