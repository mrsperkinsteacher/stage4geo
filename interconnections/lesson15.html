📋 Summary of Lesson

Today you carry out real fieldwork. You write a hypothesis first, then check the 'Made in ...' labels on 15 everyday items, record each country and its region, tally the results and draw a column graph. You finish by writing conclusions that use your own numbers, plus the limits of what you found.

This matters because geographers do not only read about the world, they measure it. Your own data, called primary data, shows how a single classroom is connected to factories and workers in many countries. Results differ between classes, and that is part of what makes fieldwork interesting.

📖 Glossary

Fieldwork - collecting information first-hand, outside the textbook, by observing and recording what is really there.
Primary data - information you collect yourself, such as by counting or measuring.
Hypothesis - a prediction you can test with data. You write it before you collect anything.
Country of origin - the country where a product was made. Labels often say 'Made in ...'.
Tally - a count made with marks as you go, in groups of five.
Conclusion - a statement that answers your question and uses your data as evidence.

💡 Did You Know?

In 2025 Australia bought about A$130 billion worth of goods and services from China. That is about 1 in every 5 dollars Australia spent on imports.

Source: Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade (DFAT), Australia's goods and services exports and imports, calendar year 2025 (China, 19.8 per cent of imports).

🗺️ Where in the World?

Dhaka, Bangladesh - the centre of one of the world's biggest clothing industries. In 2024 Bangladesh exported about US$38.5 billion of clothing, about 6.9 per cent of the world total, second only to China (about 29.6 per cent). Check your own clothing labels and you may find it there.

Sources: Prothom Alo, 10 July 2025, reporting World Trade Organization figures for 2024.
