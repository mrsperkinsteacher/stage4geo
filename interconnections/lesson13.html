Lesson 13: Trade Flows: Mapping Australia's Trade

Australia sells to the world and buys from it. Trade has a shape on the map, and most of it points north to Asia.

📋 Summary of Lesson

Today you learn what a trade flow is and the difference between exports and imports, and between goods and services. The class builds a trade flow wall, running string from Australia to its trading partners, then describes the pattern using three lenses: direction, volume (size) and distance.

This matters because trade links you to people you will never meet. Australia mostly sells raw materials such as iron ore, coal and gas, and buys finished products such as cars and phones. Most of that trade travels north to nearby Asia, with China the biggest partner.

📖 Glossary

Trade flow - the movement of goods or services from one country to another.
Export - something a country sells to another country. It flows OUT (exit = export).
Import - something a country buys from another country. It flows IN.
Trading partner - a country that another country regularly buys from and sells to.
Goods and services - goods are things you can touch (iron ore, cars, wheat); services are things done for you (education, tourism, streaming).
Spatial pattern - the shape trade makes on a map: which direction it goes, how big it is and how far it travels.

💡 Did You Know?

In 2025 Australia's biggest export was iron ore, worth about A$121 billion. China bought about 29 per cent of everything Australia sold overseas, more than any other country.

Source: Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade (DFAT), Australia's goods and services exports and imports, calendar year 2025.

📰 In the News

In December 2024 China removed the last of its trade restrictions on Australian goods, and live rock lobsters went back on sale in China. Barley, wine and timber had already returned. The Australian Government said the restrictions had affected trade worth about $20 billion.

Source: Minister for Trade and Tourism, 'Australian produce in high demand for Lunar New Year Celebrations in China', media release, 2025.

🗺️ Where in the World?

Port Hedland, Western Australia - the world's largest bulk export port by tonnage. About 573.6 million tonnes left the port in 2023-24, and 98.5 per cent of it was iron ore. Iron ore is Australia's single biggest export.

Sources: Pilbara Ports, Port of Port Hedland fact sheet; DFAT, Australia's goods and services exports and imports, 2025.

💼 Careers Connected to This Lesson

Trade analyst - studies trade data to spot patterns, such as which countries buy the most from Australia, and advises governments and businesses.

Border Force officer - checks goods coming into and leaving Australia at ports and airports, so they are declared correctly and are safe.
