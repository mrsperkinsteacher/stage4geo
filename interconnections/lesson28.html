📋 Summary of Lesson

Today you look back over the whole unit. You revisit the unit's 12 key terms, then fill in a capacity matrix: for each part of the unit you tick one honest level, from information ('I've heard of it') to wisdom ('I could teach and judge with it'), and name the evidence in your book. You finish by setting two specific goals for what to strengthen.

This matters because an honest tick is more useful than a perfect-looking one. A lower level is information, not failure: it shows you exactly where to spend your time. 'Redo my graphing worksheet on Saturday' is a goal you can act on; 'study more' is not.

📖 Glossary

Information - 'I've heard of it.'
Knowledge - 'I can explain it.'
Know-how - 'I can do it.'
Wisdom - 'I could teach and judge with it.'
Evidence - proof of what you can do, such as a worksheet, a graph or a paragraph.

💡 Did You Know?

Teaching students to plan, check and review their own learning adds about 7 months of extra progress over a year, on average. That finding is based on 246 studies, and it costs very little to do. A capacity matrix is one way of checking your own learning.

Source: Evidence for Learning, Teaching and Learning Toolkit, 'Metacognition and self-regulation', checked October 2026.

📰 In the News

UN Trade and Development (UNCTAD) reported in April 2026 that world trade in goods and services reached about US$35 trillion in 2025, up almost 7.5 per cent on 2024. It expects growth to slow during 2026 because of conflict in the Middle East, shipping problems in the Strait of Hormuz, rising prices and higher trade costs. The interconnections you studied this term are still changing.

Source: UNCTAD, Global Trade Update, April 2026.

🗺️ Where in the World?

China - Australia's biggest trading partner. In 2025 Australia sold China goods and services worth about A$196 billion and bought about A$130 billion from it, together roughly a quarter of all Australia's trade. China matters to the rest of the world too: in 2024 it was the top source of imported goods for around 40 per cent of countries.

Sources: DFAT, Australia's goods and services exports and imports, 2025; Our World in Data, 9 December 2025. The 'roughly a quarter' share is worked out from DFAT's totals.
