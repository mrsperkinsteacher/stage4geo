📋 Summary of Lesson

Today you define globalisation and learn a three-step way to analyse any technology: what it connects people to, one benefit, and one risk. You apply it to technologies such as undersea cables, GPS, online marketplaces and contactless payments, then write a short paragraph explaining one of them.

This matters because technology is the engine of globalisation. It connects people to goods, services, information, places and each other - and it can fail, as the story of Tonga's broken cable shows.

📖 Glossary

Globalisation - the increasing movement of goods, services, money, people and ideas around the world.
Undersea cable - a fibre-optic cable on the ocean floor that carries internet data between countries - about 99% of international data travels this way.
GPS - Global Positioning System - satellites that let devices work out exactly where they are on Earth.
Online marketplace - a website or app where buyers and sellers from anywhere in the world can trade with each other.
Contactless payment - paying by tapping a card or phone, with the money moving electronically between banks in seconds.

💡 Did You Know?

The International Telecommunication Union (ITU), the United Nations agency for communications, says undersea cables carry over 99 per cent of international data exchanges. Other sources give a slightly lower figure (some say over 95 per cent), but either way, it is almost all of it. Each cable is roughly the width of a garden hose, with glass fibres as thin as a human hair inside.

Sources: ITU press release, 29 November 2024; PreventionWeb (republished from The Conversation), 17 January 2022; ABC News, 6 March 2024.

📰 In the News

On 6 September 2025, several undersea cables in the Red Sea near Jeddah, Saudi Arabia, were cut. Internet users in the United Arab Emirates, India and Pakistan reported slower connections. Microsoft said traffic passing through the Middle East could be slower, and rerouted it along other paths. Repairs to undersea cables can take weeks to months.

Source: Telecom Review, 'International cable breaks in Red Sea cause latency surge across Asia and Gulf', 8 September 2025.

🗺️ Where in the World?

Tonga - after a volcanic eruption and tsunami in January 2022, Tonga's main undersea cable was broken and the country was cut off from the world's internet for more than five weeks, using only makeshift satellite links. The cable was reconnected on 22 February 2022 after about 90 km of damaged cable was replaced.

Source: Al Jazeera, 'Tonga internet reconnected five weeks after volcanic eruption', 23 February 2022.

💼 Careers Connected to This Lesson

Telecommunications engineer - designs and looks after the networks, including undersea cables, that carry phone calls and internet data.

Cable ship technician - works on ships that lay and repair cables on the ocean floor, as happened when Tonga's cable was replaced.
