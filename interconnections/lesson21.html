Lesson 21: Counting the Cost

One cheap t-shirt brings good and bad effects to people and places far away. Find out who gains and who pays.

📋 Summary of Lesson

Today you sort the impacts of the t-shirt trade into three groups: environmental, sociocultural and economic. You look at dye wastewater, textile waste, garment jobs in Bangladesh and an estimate of where the money from one shirt goes. Then you write a structured PEEL paragraph about how one good connects people and places. This paragraph is the inquiry checkpoint for the week.

This matters because trade is rarely just good or just bad. The same factory can give thousands of people paid work and also pollute a river that farmers depend on. A good geographer weighs both sides and asks who carries the costs and who keeps the gains.

📖 Glossary

Environmental impact - an effect on the natural world: water, air, rivers, land and climate.
Sociocultural impact - an effect on people, communities and their ways of life.
Economic impact - an effect on money, jobs, businesses and trade.
Interconnection - the ways people and places depend on and affect one another.
Globalisation - the process linking the world's people, businesses and countries ever more closely together.
Scale - the size or level you look at, from one farm up to about 2 billion shirts a year.

💡 Did You Know?

In 2018-19 Australia produced about 780,000 tonnes of textile waste, and only about 7 per cent of it was recycled. Most of the rest went to landfill.

Source: CHOICE, 'Is Australia waking up to its textile waste problem?', 16 August 2021.

📰 In the News

8 October 2026: Added 15/11 Greenpeace Africa collected 201 thrown-away garments from three sites in Accra, Ghana, including James Town Beach and Kantamanto market, and found labels from brands such as Primark, Marks & Spencer and H&M. Greenpeace and its partners plan to give Ghana's government a petition signed by more than 25,000 people, asking for protection from unusable clothing imports and for brands to take responsibility for the waste - an example of an environmental cost of fast fashion that is carried far from where the clothes were bought.

Source: Greenpeace Africa, 8 October 2026, "Greenpeace reveals brands found in Ghana's textile waste as anti fast fashion installation is unveiled."

21 September 2026: Added 15/11 RMIT University researchers interviewed 20 Australian millennials and found they know fast fashion harms the environment but often keep buying because it is cheap and convenient. A Roy Morgan survey found 5 million Australians bought from Temu and 2.9 million bought from Shein. The researchers suggest showing the 'cost per wear' of clothes to help shoppers compare cheap and long-lasting items - a reminder that shoppers' choices are part of who carries the costs of clothing.

Source: RMIT University, 21 September 2026, "Shoppers know fast fashion harms environment but keep buying: study."

World Trade Organization data show Bangladesh was the world's second largest clothing exporter in 2024, behind China. It exported garments worth about US$38.5 billion, or about 6.9 per cent of the world market. China exported about US$165 billion.

Source: The Daily Star, republished by Asia News Network, 9 July 2025.

🗺️ Where in the World?

Gazipur, Bangladesh - a district near Dhaka with 1,222 garment factories employing about 1.6 million people. Factories in the area have discharged dye wastewater into rivers such as the Balu, and farmers along the river report damaged fields.

Sources: Dialogue Earth, 12 January 2023.

💼 Careers Connected to This Lesson

Sustainable fashion designer - designs clothes that last longer, use less water and dye, and can be repaired or recycled.

Environmental scientist - tests river water near factories and farms and reports on pollution, which helps governments set and enforce rules.
