📋 Summary of Lesson

Today you trace one good, a cotton t-shirt, through its whole supply chain, from a cotton farm to the day it is thrown away. You watch a short animated video, look at photos of two very different ends of the chain, and use an atlas to find the real places involved and their direction from Australia.

This matters because the tag on a shirt usually names only one country. Behind it are farmers, mill workers, dyers, sewers, sailors and shop staff in many places. All of them are connected to the person who wears the shirt.

📖 Glossary

Supply chain - every step, place and person involved in making a product and getting it to the person who buys it.
Raw material - the natural product everything starts from. For a t-shirt, it is cotton.
Garment - a single item of clothing.
Retail - selling products to the people who will use them, in shops and online.
Disposal - what happens to a product when we finish with it: landfill, donation, recycling or export as used clothing.

🌍 Link to the SDGs

SDG 12 - Responsible Consumption and Production

Goal 12 includes cutting waste through prevention, reduction, recycling and reuse. In 2018-19 only 7 per cent of Australia's textile waste was recycled.
SDG 6 - Clean Water and Sanitation

Growing cotton and dyeing fabric both use water, and factory waste can pollute rivers.
SDG 8 - Decent Work and Economic Growth

Millions of people earn a living along the clothing supply chain, from farms to factories to shops.

💡 Did You Know?

In an average year, Australian cotton growers produce enough cotton to clothe 500 million people. That comes from up to 1,500 cotton farms, and more than 7,000 people work in the industry.

Source: Cotton Australia, Industry overview (jobs figure from its 2023 Sustainability Update).

📰 In the News

Ready-made garments are Australia's key import from Bangladesh. They come in duty-free and quota-free because Bangladesh is a Least Developed Country. Bangladesh is due to move out of that group in November 2026, and Australia has said the duty-free, quota-free access will continue after that.

Source: Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade, Bangladesh country brief.

🗺️ Where in the World?

Gazipur, Bangladesh - a district near Dhaka with 1,222 garment factories employing about 1.6 million workers. Waste water from factories flows into local rivers such as the Turag, and farmers along nearby rivers report damaged fields and fewer fish.

Source: Dialogue Earth (2023).

💼 Careers Connected to This Lesson

Cotton agronomist - works with cotton growers on soil, water and pests, helping them grow good crops with less water.

Textile recycling specialist - finds ways to sort, reuse and recycle old clothes so fewer end up in landfill.
