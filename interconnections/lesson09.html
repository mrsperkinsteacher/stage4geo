📋 Summary of Lesson

Today you compare transport access in Japan and Australia using a fact file, then make a written judgement about whether access to transport is fair. A scaffold helps you reach a judgement in three levels, using the sentence pattern 'Although..., ..., therefore transport access is / is not fair because...'.

This matters because the same transport technology does not reach everyone equally. Geographers describe that gap as inequality, and weighing both sides of an argument before you decide is a skill you will use in every Geography lesson from now on.

📖 Glossary

Accessibility - how easy it is for people to reach the goods, services and places they need.
Remote - far away from major towns and services.
Infrastructure - the big structures a country builds - roads, railways, ports, airports.
Freight - goods carried by trucks, trains, ships or planes.
Inequality - when some people or places have much better access or opportunities than others.

💡 Did You Know?

Japan's Shinkansen opened on 1 October 1964 between Tokyo and Osaka and is described as the world's first high-speed rail service. Early trains reached about 210 km/h and the trip took four hours. The fastest trains now reach about 320 km/h, and over its first fifty years the Shinkansen carried more than 10 billion passengers.

Source: Embassy of Japan in the UK, 'Shinkansen' (2014).

📰 In the News

In February 2026 the Australian Government committed $659.6 million to a two-year development phase for a high-speed rail link between Newcastle and Sydney. The business case, prepared by the High Speed Rail Authority, aims for a trip of around one hour from Newcastle to Central Sydney.

Source: Infrastructure Magazine, 25 February 2026.
